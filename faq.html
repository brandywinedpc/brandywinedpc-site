Insurance & cost

I already have health insurance. Isn't this paying twice?

They pay for different things. Your insurance is for the expensive and the unexpected - a hospital stay, a surgery, an MRI, a specialist. Keep it. It's genuinely good at that.

Your membership pays for the relationship: the visits, the texts at 8pm about a rash, the annual physical that doesn't feel like a conveyor belt. Most members find they lean on the rest of their plan less, because problems get handled early instead of in an urgent care waiting room at nine on a Saturday.

What does it cost?

$150 a month for an individual, $200 for a couple, $300 for a family of two adults plus every child under 18, and $150 for an adult 65 or older. Month to month, no contract. New members pay a one-time $100 enrollment fee - waived for the first 150 founding members.

What if I can't afford it?

Then say so. Morgan would genuinely rather have the conversation than have you quietly decide this practice isn't for people like you - that's the opposite of why she's building it.

She can't make it free, and she won't pretend the price is nothing. But there is often more room than people expect, and it costs nothing to ask.

Can I pay with an HSA or FSA?

The tax treatment of direct primary care fees has shifted in recent years and depends on your specific plan and situation, so we won't give you a confident yes or no on a website. Ask your plan administrator or your tax preparer - and if it would change your decision, ask Morgan and she'll tell you what she knows.

Medicare & age

I'm on Medicare. How does this work?

Medicare keeps working exactly as it does now for your hospital care, your specialists, your labs, and your imaging. None of that changes, and you should absolutely keep it.

Dr. Katz opts out of Medicare billing for her own services only. That's the piece that makes the time possible - she isn't shaping your visit around what a billing code will reimburse. You pay her directly for her care; Medicare handles everything else.

Practically, this means signing a short private agreement acknowledging that Medicare won't be billed for her services. She'll walk you through it in plain English before you sign anything.

I'm over 65 and keep getting turned away. Will you take me?

Yes. Plainly: yes.

A lot of practices treat patients over 65 as expensive and complicated, and a lot of long-standing patients have found themselves without a doctor because their physician retired or their practice got absorbed by a health system. That is a bad thing to have happen to you, and it's a large part of why this practice exists.

You will pay the same rate as anyone else - $150 a month, or $200 for a couple. There is no age surcharge.

Do you take children?

Yes. Dr. Katz is board-certified in family medicine, which covers newborns through adults - that's the whole appeal of the specialty. The family membership includes two adults and every child under 18 at one flat rate, however many children that is.

How the practice works

Is this the same as concierge medicine?

The access and the relationship are similar. The structure isn't. Concierge practices typically charge a retainer and bill your insurance for each visit on top of it.

Here there's one number, it's published on this website, and there's no billing layer underneath it. Nothing against the other model - it's just a more complicated way to arrive at the same conversation.

What if I need a specialist or hospital care?

Membership doesn't replace that care - it coordinates it, which is arguably where the value is highest.

Dr. Katz refers you to a specialist she'd send her own family to, sends actual clinical notes rather than a fax nobody reads, and follows up after the appointment so the plan doesn't evaporate in the gap between two offices. Your insurance covers the specialist and the hospital themselves.

Can I really just text my doctor?

Yes, and members use it constantly - a photo of a rash, a question about a dose, a "is this worth coming in for." It goes to Dr. Katz, not to a triage nurse or a portal queue.

Two honest caveats. She's a person with a family, so a message at midnight gets answered in the morning unless it's urgent. And anything genuinely emergent - chest pain, trouble breathing, stroke symptoms - should go to 911, not to a text message. She'll say the same thing.

How many patients will she have?

About 500 patients total, once the panel is full - a small fraction of the 1,800+ she carried at ChristianaCare. The exact cap is what makes same-day access possible rather than aspirational, and once the practice reaches it, it closes to a waitlist.

This is the one genuinely non-negotiable part of the model. A direct primary care practice that keeps taking patients past its cap is just a regular practice with a subscription fee.

What if I want to cancel?

Then you cancel. It's month to month, there's no contract and no minimum term, and nobody will make it difficult or ask you to sit through a retention call.

The only real consideration is that if you want to come back later, it depends on whether there's room in the panel at that point.

What happens when Dr. Katz is away?

She takes vacations and occasionally gets sick, like anyone. There is coverage arranged for those stretches and members are told in advance who it is and how to reach them.

A one-physician practice is the source of most of what's good about this model and it is also its main limitation. It would be dishonest to present it any other way.

Where will the office be?

In the Hockessin / Greenville / Limestone corridor of New Castle County. The specific address is being finalized, which is part of why the waitlist asks for your ZIP code - Morgan is using it to choose a location that's convenient for the people actually joining.

Waitlist members hear the address first, before it goes on the website.
