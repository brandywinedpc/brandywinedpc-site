Why this practice exists

Two thousand patients is a directory. Four hundred is a practice.

Morgan trained in family medicine at Jefferson Health in Philadelphia, finishing as chief resident, and went straight from residency to ChristianaCare. She spent four years there as an attending. She was good at it - her panel grew past two thousand patients and had to close to new ones inside of a year.

That is a lot of people to be responsible for. It is not a lot of time per person. The math of insurance-based primary care means fifteen-minute slots, a full inbox at nine at night, and the quiet knowledge that you are doing a decent job for far too many people instead of a genuinely good one for a reasonable number.

So she is building the other thing. A capped panel, paid for directly, with no billing codes deciding how long a conversation gets to be. Fewer patients, more medicine.

How direct primary care works

You pay the doctor. That's the whole model.

No insurance company sits between you and Dr. Katz - which means no copays for her visits, no billing codes shaping your appointment, and no one but her deciding how long you get.

A flat monthly fee

One predictable payment, month to month. No contract, no enrollment fee, cancel whenever you like. You always know exactly what her care costs, because it's on this website.

Real access, not a phone tree

Text or call Dr. Katz directly - her, not a triage nurse. Same or next-day appointments when you're sick. Visits that run as long as the problem takes.

Keep your insurance

Membership pays for her care, not for hospitals, specialists, labs, or imaging. Keep your plan for those - that's what it's genuinely good at.

Fair questions

The things people ask before they join.

I already have insurance. Isn't this paying twice?

They pay for different things. Your insurance is for the expensive and the unexpected - a hospital stay, a surgery, an MRI, a specialist. Keep it. It's genuinely good at that.

Your membership pays for the relationship: the visits, the texts at 8pm about a rash, the annual physical that doesn't feel like a conveyor belt. Most members find they use the rest of their plan less, because problems get handled early instead of in an urgent care waiting room.

I'm on Medicare. How does this work?

Medicare keeps working exactly as it does now for your hospital care, your specialists, your labs, and your imaging. None of that changes.

Dr. Katz opts out of Medicare billing for her own services only. That's what makes the time possible - she isn't structuring your visit around what a billing code will reimburse. You pay her directly for her care; Medicare handles everything else.

Is this the same as concierge medicine?

The access and the relationship are similar. The structure isn't. Concierge practices typically charge a retainer and bill your insurance for each visit on top of it.

Here there's one number, it's published on this website, and there's no billing layer underneath it. Nothing against the other model - it's just a more complicated way to arrive at the same conversation.

What if I can't afford it?

Then say so. Morgan would genuinely rather have the conversation than have you quietly decide this practice isn't for people like you - that's the opposite of why she's building it.

She can't make it free, and she won't pretend the price is nothing. But there is often more room than people expect, and it costs nothing to ask.
