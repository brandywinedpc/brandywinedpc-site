How it works

Direct primary care, explained without the jargon.

Here is the entire model in one sentence: you pay your doctor a flat monthly fee, and in exchange she keeps her practice small enough to actually take care of you. Everything below is just detail.

You join

Pick the membership that matches your household and start paying monthly. No enrollment fee, no contract, no minimum term. If it isn't working, you cancel - the way you'd cancel anything else.

You get her directly

You get Dr. Katz's phone number. Text her, call her, or send a photo of the rash. Same or next-day appointments when you need to be seen. Video visits when you don't need to drive anywhere.

You keep your insurance

Membership pays for Dr. Katz's care only. Keep your plan for hospitals, specialists, surgery, imaging, and prescriptions - that is what insurance is genuinely good at.

The part that surprises people

Nothing about your insurance has to change.

Membership sits alongside whatever coverage you already carry. It doesn't replace it, conflict with it, or require you to drop it.

What changes day to day

Same medicine. Very different experience.

When you're sick

You text Dr. Katz in the morning and she answers. If you need to be seen, you're seen that day or the next. There's no triage line deciding whether your problem qualifies.

At your physical

The visit runs as long as it needs to - often thirty to sixty minutes. There is time to go through your labs properly and time for the question you weren't sure was worth asking.

Between visits

Most things don't need an appointment at all. A photo, a question about a medication, a "should I be worried about this" - handled by text, usually the same day.

When you need a specialist

Dr. Katz finds the right one, sends actual clinical notes rather than a fax, and follows up afterward so the plan doesn't fall through the gap between offices.

When you travel

Your doctor is a phone call away regardless of where you are. For a lot of members this turns out to be the thing they value most.

When you're perfectly fine

You use it less, and that's fine too. Membership isn't priced on visit volume - it's the cost of having a doctor who knows you and is reachable.

Being straight with you

What direct primary care doesn't do

Every honest description of this model needs this section, so here it is.

It is not insurance, and it isn't a substitute for it. Membership pays for Dr. Katz's care. It does not pay for a hospitalization, a surgery, an MRI, or an ambulance. If you drop your coverage and something serious happens, a monthly membership will not help you with that bill. Please keep a plan.

It's an additional cost. You're paying for membership on top of premiums you're already paying. For some households the value is obvious and for others it genuinely isn't. We'd rather you work that out honestly than sign up and regret it.

One doctor is one doctor. Dr. Katz takes vacations and gets sick like anyone else. There is coverage arranged for those stretches, and she'll tell you plainly who it is - but a small practice means a small practice.

It doesn't make you a better historian of your own health. The model works because you use it. Members who text when something feels off get enormously more out of this than members who wait for an annual physical out of habit.
